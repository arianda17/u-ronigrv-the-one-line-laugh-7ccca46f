# ENTRY — The One-Line Laugh

## Winning line

I bought a $14 salad and the app asked if I wanted to round up for charity — yes, please round it down to free.

Character count: 111 (UTF-8, single sentence, 1–200 characters, not only whitespace — passes the gate).

## Losing variants (and why each was rejected)

1. "My bank app congratulates me on saving money while charging me $12 for not having enough money." — everyday truth, but the irony is stated flatly: no turn at the end, and bank-fee jokes are well-worn.
2. "I finally cleared my inbox at 3am, which is exactly when no one needs anything from me." — true but gentle; lacks a real surprise and a genuine laugh.
3. "Self-checkout saves time: I now spend twenty minutes arguing with a machine for free." — decent punch, but self-checkout frustration is a familiar observation, so it feels less fresh.
4. "I signed up for a free trial, forgot to cancel, and now a company I never use knows my card better than I do." — common experience; the ending fizzles instead of landing a turn.

## Why the winner fits the judge criteria

- Real surprise at the end that still makes sense: the round-up-for-charity prompt is answered by flipping it — "round it down to free" — a turn the reader does not see coming but that parses cleanly once it arrives.
- Everyday truth people recognise: overpriced salads and guilt-trip round-up prompts are universally experienced.
- Economy of words: 111 characters, no filler — every word earns its place and nothing explains the joke.
- Fresh: not a clichéd airplane-food, generic-wife, or dad-joke line.
